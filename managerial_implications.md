# Explicit Managerial Implications

Each: the CONDITION (when it applies), the ACTION, and the structural result it rests on.

MI1 — Diagnose before you decarbonize.
  Measure two carbon-intensity-per-dollar ratios: holding eta/h vs inbound transport eps/t, and last-mile
  eps_L/t_L vs facilities phi/F. These two numbers locate you in the 2x2 and dictate the DIRECTION carbon
  should move speed and density. Reflexively consolidating or slow-steaming is wrong roughly half the time.
  [Rests on: P2, P3 thresholds.]

MI2 — Carbon can mean ship FASTER, not slower.
  CONDITION: inventory is carbon-heavy relative to its cost (eta/h > eps/t) — refrigerated, volatile,
  high-service goods. ACTION: a tighter carbon cap should make you SPEED UP (cut dirty safety stock),
  not slow-steam. Above the threshold, each increment of carbon price raises the optimal speed.
  [Rests on: P2 / Theorem 2 — overturns slow-steaming orthodoxy.]

MI3 — Consolidation's green benefit is pooling + facility energy, NOT transport.
  Centralizing LENGTHENS the last mile and RAISES transport carbon; its genuine carbon saving comes from
  inventory risk pooling and lower facility energy. CONDITION: if last-mile is your dirty bucket
  (eps_L/t_L > phi/F), consolidating RAISES total emissions. ACTION: stop citing "consolidate to cut
  transport emissions" — measure the buckets first. [Rests on: P3 / Theorem 3.]

MI4 — A carbon program is a network REDESIGN, not a dial-tune.
  Because density and speed are complements, moving one without the other backfires or leaves savings on
  the table. ACTION: re-optimize the number of DCs AND the transport mode jointly; expect a discrete shift
  in design, not a marginal tweak. [Rests on: P1 / complementarity.]

MI5 — Segment the portfolio; do not green the whole chain one way.
  Different products sit in different quadrants. ACTION: volatile / cold-chain / high-service -> faster +
  more central; bulky / low-value / dirty-last-mile -> more decentralized. A single chain-wide green policy
  is wrong for part of the assortment. [Rests on: comparative statics in sigma, alpha, eps_L.]

MI6 — Read your implied carbon shadow price from the design gap.
  A carbon price tau and an emission cap B give the SAME optimal design at matched stringency (lambda=tau).
  ACTION: how far your green network departs from the cost-optimal one reveals your implied internal carbon
  price; widening that gap = a rising effective tau. [Rests on: price/cap duality.]

MI7 — Service promises and demand variability are carbon levers.
  Higher service alpha or higher volatility sigma make inventory carbon-heavier, pushing toward faster +
  more central. ACTION: a 99% fill-rate promise carries a COMPUTABLE carbon cost; reducing sigma
  (forecasting, demand pooling) is itself a decarbonization lever. [Rests on: v*, n* monotone in z(alpha), sigma.]

MI8 — Policy: cap TOTAL logistics carbon, not just transport.
  A transport-only cap (common in regulation) pushes firms to hold more inventory (warehouse carbon often
  uncapped) -> intra-firm leakage; a tighter transport cap can RAISE total firm emissions. ACTION (regulator):
  target total logistics carbon. [Rests on: two-bucket structure; transport-cap-raises-holding mechanism.]
