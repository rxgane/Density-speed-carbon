Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Cost, Carbon, and the Speed of Replenishment in Distribution
Network Design,'' for publication in \emph{OR Spectrum}.

The manuscript develops a quantitative decision model for carbon-induced operational substitution in
distribution network design. A firm chooses replenishment speed and network density to minimize cost subject
to a carbon cap and a service requirement. These two decisions provide two services: availability, through
safety stock or replenishment speed, and proximity, through facilities or last-mile travel. Carbon accounting
can select a different operational activity than cost minimization because each substitution reduces one
source of emissions while increasing another.

The scientific contribution is a set of closed-form decision thresholds for this joint speed--density design
problem. The speed threshold compares holding carbon per dollar with inbound-transport carbon per dollar; the
density threshold compares last-mile carbon per dollar with a pooling-adjusted combination of facility and
holding intensities; and the complementarity result shows why the two decisions should be evaluated jointly.
The practical contribution is a diagnostic that managers can compute from activity-level cost and carbon rate
data to classify products and assess carbon caps, service requirements, and accounting boundaries. The paper
therefore fits \emph{OR Spectrum}'s emphasis on quantitative approaches to operational decision-making with
clearly articulated contributions to science and practice.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere.
